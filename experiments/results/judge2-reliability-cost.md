# Judge 2: reliability and cost review of the four Jev-only synthesis designs (2026-09-20)

Lens: which design wastes the fewest Jev calls and test runs, degrades gracefully, never gets stuck, and
stays under the budgets (`docs/JEV-ONLY.md`: >= 60 % of QuixBugs under $0.05 and 2 minutes per program;
run-level spend cap and wall time of the engine). Live Jev spend for this review: **$0.00**. Every number
below is quoted from a file under `experiments/results/` or recomputed from its JSONL by
`experiments/judge2/recompute.py` (code only, 1 s). Designs read in full:
`experiments/designs/{repair-search,test-driven-decomposition,grammar-synthesis,contrarian}.md`.

## 2. Scores (0-10 on the reliability-and-cost lens)

| Rank | Design | Score | Justification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **test-driven-decomposition** | **8.0** | The tightest and most graceful budgets of the four: 16 test runs and 30 requests per step, a `run` proposal (one cheap outer step) when the budget hits mid-search, and a ledger that parks a sub-goal after exhaustion or two budget-hit steps and re-opens it only when a committed change touches its file, so no goal is retried blindly. Test runs are goal-subset runs (the failing tests plus the same file's passing tests), which is the one lever that makes SWE-bench steps affordable (module runs are 5-60 s; `coverage-study.md` gives 1,641 depth-1 mutants per line, and the design shrinks that pool first by restricting identifier substitution to the enclosing function's names, median 9 vs 116). It keeps every measured rule the prototype's failures point at: beam of bases instead of greedy commitment (3 trap failures over two prototype runs), unchanged line never a candidate (20/20 test-passing top picks, `probe-question-design.md`), progress computed in code (240/240, `probe-progress-judgment.md`), `fixProbablyAbsent` skips test runs (AUROC 0.916). Resume is the most durable: goals live in `plan.remaining` in a fixed grammar and are re-derived from a re-run baseline. Deductions: `edit_class` wording and traceback-based goal clustering are unmeasured (both soft, both cost a request per step); the K_VERIFY = 3 justification cites full-criteria Nouls (39/40 top-3 at every N) while the design uses compact Nouls above N = 60, where top-3 is 36/40 at N = 254; and "the three trap cases all had a better candidate still in the round-1 queue" is contradicted for `kth` (fix at Choice rank 182/189 in `prototype-baseline.md`), so its own `kth` prediction of "unlikely" is the honest one. |
| 2 | **repair-search** | **7.5** | Most completely grounded design: every Jev question except the cluster guard is one a probe asked with that wording, and the guard never overrides tests (a passing candidate is proposed even when Q-V1 is low). Spends Jev to avoid test runs where the measurement says so: `fixProbablyAbsent` before any run, Noul order at N > 10, verify every p >= 0.5 else top-3, insert sites top-3 regardless (gold statements at 0.33-0.35), global queue across sites keyed by (base passed, p) so `shortest_path_length`'s rank-10 winner is reached early. Predicted QuixBugs cost $0.003-0.008 and 10-30 s per program is an order of magnitude under the criterion and consistent with the prototype's measured $0.00088 / 3.2 s plus the ~4x token cost of Noul reranking. Deductions: heaviest Jev spender on SWE (~70-80 requests per step, ~11 chunks x 5 anchors, still only $0.04-0.06); the "task impossible" exit is a deliberate three-step stall (P-DONE-FAIL blocked by `plan_mismatch` three times to trip the loop detector) rather than a first-class stop; per-step caps are looser (40 runs, 180 s); `--resume` depends on the runner's JSON line surviving the 600-char window tail; SWE ranking at 1,641 candidates and the beam-of-bases remedy are unmeasured (both acknowledged with costed experiments, ~$0.55 total). |
| 3 | **grammar-synthesis** | **6.0** | Its reach layer is cheap and actually measured: one sketch request per site at $0.00019 and 238 ms, 39/40 shape coverage, and it fires only after concrete seeds fail, so the typical program costs the same as the prototype. Budgets are explicit (60 requests, 24 runs, 90 s per step) and the K = 5 widening on P(top) < 0.5 or P(escape) >= 0.3 routes low confidence to tests instead of trusting the order (13/40 programs in the pilot). Deductions on this lens: sketch precision is low (top-3 29-31/40) and each kept sketch fans out to B = 3 fills, so the reach step is where test runs and requests accumulate (10-14 requests, 7-10 runs); the acceptance gate is an unmeasured Noul threshold used as a hard rule ("Q-GENUINE >= 0.7 -> accept; < 0.3 -> keep searching one more round"; `done` requires >= 0.7 or two agreeing clusters) and the nearest measured analogues put the gold below 0.7 on 4/10 and 7/14 programs and below 0.3 on `quicksort` (`contrarian-arbitrate.*.jsonl`; `lit-search-based-repair.md` Noul p(true fix) 0.29-0.88), so the synthesizer would burn extra rounds or withhold `done` on 30-50 % of genuine fixes until the outer completion Noul rescues it; the token-beam fallback costs ~31 requests and 3.3 s per line for 20/40 reach and is entered on a budget test (`requests >= 35`) rather than a measured signal. |
| 4 | **contrarian (Sieve)** | **5.0** | Wins the "fewest Jev calls" sub-criterion outright (1-3 requests per QuixBugs program, ~$0.005 for the benchmark) and contributes the best-measured overfit instrument (arbitration among test-passing candidates 10/10 and 13/14 gold-or-equivalent for $0.0008-0.0015). But on the other half of the lens it is the most wasteful by its own JSONL: 4,892 runs and 1,808 CPU-s at the *known* true line (9.9x the prototype's estimated test CPU, 11.9x its runs), 37,243 runs and 13,783 CPU-s for the `widened` phase (75.5x), against a Jev saving of about $0.03 for the whole benchmark; the text's "about 3x the prototype's test CPU" is contradicted by these files. Wall time is within the 2-minute criterion but not by much for the widened phase (`mergesort` 119 s, `levenshtein` 75 s, plus the first phase). The graceful-degradation story on SWE-bench is that it becomes the ranked design it criticises with a slower oracle (acknowledged in risk 1). The overfit guard has a hole (Fatal flaws 2-3): the pseudo-code proposes a single-cluster plausible set with no Jev question, so the one measured all-overfit set (`depth_first_search`, 7 behaviourally identical candidates) would be proposed, while the stated Noul < 0.3 rejection rule would reject `quicksort`'s gold (0.15). Insertion reach relies on exhaustive testing of 300-700 statement templates per gap x top-3 gaps, never measured (brute force in Table 0.2 left 3 of 4 insertion bugs with 0 plausible candidates), and the `deep` phase's second-order pools are 5-10k lines per site (`coverage-study.md`), i.e. minutes, not the "10-20 s step" claimed. |

## 3. Best ideas worth grafting from the non-winners

1. **Contrarian: exhaust instead of rank when a test run is cheap.** At the true line the whole first-order set runs in a median 3.8 s at 8-way parallelism and the gold is the only passer on 25/36 replacements; make the ranker's job "order the queue" and let a run-cost budget (`floor(step_seconds x parallelism / t_run)`) decide how many of the ranked candidates run, so QuixBugs-size suites verify the whole top site and SWE-size suites verify the top 3.
2. **Contrarian: adaptive per-test timeout `clamp(3 x baseline, 0.5 s, 2 s)`.** The 0.5 s setting rejected no gold fix on 35/35 enumerated replacements and cut the timeout-dominated programs (`sqrt` 47.6 s -> 31.9 s, `bitcount` 24.7 s -> 24.2 s at 12-way with far more candidates).
3. **Contrarian: behaviour clustering before any arbitration, and arbitration only among passers.** Cluster the test-passing candidates on perturbed inputs, propose the min-edit member when one cluster, ask `Q_arbitrate` + paired `Q_general` Nouls only when clusters disagree (10/10 and 13/14 gold-or-equivalent, $0.0001 per request); treat "escape >= 0.9 and max Noul < 0.1 on a plausible set" as the signal to move to gap sites (the `depth_first_search` case), but never as a rejection rule for a lone passer at the true line (`quicksort` 0.15).
4. **Contrarian: pair partials from different sites that fix disjoint failing-test subsets** (<= 10 pair runs) instead of adopting one as a base; it covers the three genuine two-step prototype repairs without the greedy trap.
5. **Grammar-synthesis: the sketch Choice as a one-request reach layer** (39/40 coverage, median 64 options, $0.00019) placed after concrete seeds fail, with K = 5 when P(top) < 0.5 or P(escape) >= 0.3, the edit-class Choice in the same request as a soft source prior (35-36/40 top-2), and "expand 1 when p(top) >= 0.9 else B = 3" for slot fills.
6. **Grammar-synthesis: overlap Jev and tests.** Issue the next round's Jev requests while the current round's candidates run; Jev is ~240 ms and the loop is test-bound.
7. **Repair-search: the global verification queue across sites** keyed by (base passed count desc, Noul p desc), the shuffle-and-average re-ask when the top-2 margin is < 0.10 and a run costs > 20 s (order sensitivity moved `kth` 0.48 -> 0.14 on a reshuffle), top-3 at insert sites regardless of p, never mutating `def` lines, SBFL top-5 anchors on single-file workspaces, and the rule that the guard never overrides tests.
8. **Repair-search: 150-candidate chunks on SWE-size states** (the 254-chunk request was 18k tokens on 10-line programs; the cap is 32k).

## 4. Fatal flaws (claims contradicted by a results file)

1. **contrarian.md §5: "spends about 3x the prototype's test CPU".** `contrarian-exhaustive.truth.jsonl` sums to 4,892 runs / 1,808 CPU-s (true line known); `prototype-baseline.jsonl` has 412 runs, about 182 CPU-s at the same per-program run times: 9.9x CPU, 11.9x runs. With K = 3 sites the factor is ~30x and the `widened` phase (`contrarian-exhaustive.all.jsonl`, 37,243 runs / 13,783 CPU-s) is 75.5x.
2. **contrarian.md §0/§3.4: "a plausible set whose best Noul is below 0.3 is not proposed".** In its own Table 0.3 the `quicksort` set's maximum Noul is 0.15 (gold) and in Table 0.4 0.49 (alternative fix) with the gold at 0.18; `find_first_in_sorted` 0.40 and `minimum_spanning_tree` 0.37 sit next to the threshold. The rule rejects a correct, only-passing fix.
3. **contrarian.md §1 pseudo-code vs §0 Table 0.4.** Phase 4 proposes `patch(min_edit(clusters[0]))` without any Jev question when `|clusters| = 1`; the seven `depth_first_search` overfits (`return any(<X> for nextnode in node.successors)`, all truthy generators) are behaviourally identical, so the "escape 0.90 rejects the all-overfit set" measurement is never consulted in the algorithm as written and the prototype's one wrong repair is reproduced.
4. **contrarian.md §6 prediction "37-39/40" and §5 "10-20 s step".** The 4 insertion bugs and `shortest_paths` need gap-site template pools of 300-700 statements x top-3 gaps and second-order pools of 5-10k lines per site (`coverage-study.md` sizes; `probe-selection.md` insertion pools 295-682), none exhaustively tested anywhere; Table 0.2 brute force left `reverse_linked_list`, `shunting_yard`, `wrap`, `shortest_paths` at 0 plausible. At 0.3 s per run and 8-way, a 5k-line `deep` site is 3+ minutes, over the 2-minute criterion.
5. **grammar-synthesis.md §3/§4.5: acceptance and `done` gated on Q-GENUINE >= 0.7, "keep searching" below 0.3.** Unmeasured, and the nearest measured analogues contradict the thresholds: `contrarian-arbitrate.truth.jsonl` puts the gold below 0.7 on 4/10 programs and at 0.15 on `quicksort`; `contrarian-arbitrate.all.jsonl` 7/14 below 0.7; `lit-search-based-repair.md` §7 Noul p(true fix) 0.29-0.88. A correct lone passer with p < 0.3 costs an extra search round and never yields a synthesizer-proposed `done`.
6. **test-driven-decomposition.md §3 and repair-search.md F4 / grammar-synthesis.md §1.5: "top-3 by Nouls holds the fix on 39/40 at every set size" as the basis for K = 3.** True for full-criteria Nouls (40/40/39/39) but all three designs switch to compact Nouls above N = 60, where `probe-selection.md` measures top-3 40/40/38/36; at N = 254 K = 3 misses 4/40, not 1/40.
7. **test-driven-decomposition.md §1.7: "the prototype's three trap cases all had a better candidate still in the round-1 queue".** `prototype-baseline.md` puts `kth`'s reference fix at Jev rank 182/189 in its line (p 0.00); finishing that queue is 180+ runs, far outside the design's 16-run step cap. (`sqrt` at rank 3 and `topological_ordering` are consistent with the claim.)
8. **repair-search.md §6.1 `kth` "likely (0.7)" from a Noul of 0.51 at N = 254.** `lit-search-based-repair.md` verification shows the same candidate moving 0.48 -> 0.14 (rank 2 -> 6) on a reshuffle; the design's own order-sensitivity rule (§1.5) only fires when a run costs > 20 s, so on QuixBugs nothing protects this prediction. Not fatal to the design, but the 34-36 band should be read as 33-36.

## 5. What this means for the design

- Adopt the **test-driven-decomposition skeleton** (ledger, parking, goal-subset runs, fixed-form plan items, regression never kept) as the outer structure; it is the only one whose per-step budget is small enough that a budget hit is a cheap `run` step rather than a stall, and the only one with a durable resume story.
- Inside a sub-goal, use **repair-search's measured ranking and queue rules** (Noul order, `fixProbablyAbsent` before runs, global queue, insert sites top-3, guard never overrides tests), and graft the **contrarian run-cost budget**: when `t_run x |candidates| / parallelism` is under the step's test wall (QuixBugs: seconds), run the whole set at the top site and skip ranking; otherwise rank and run the top 3. This removes the `ranking_missed` class where it is free and keeps the Jev lever where tests are expensive.
- Keep every Jev acceptance question **advisory**: cluster passers by behaviour in code first, ask `Q_arbitrate` only when clusters disagree, and never withhold a lone test-passing candidate on a Noul threshold (measured gold Nouls reach 0.15).
- Add the **sketch Choice** as a single-request reach round after concrete seeds fail, before the 31-request token beam.
- Fix the citation error common to three designs: size K_VERIFY from the compact-Noul curve (36/40 at N = 254), or use full-criteria Nouls when N <= 150 ($0.0014, 374 ms) where the 39/40 figure actually holds.
